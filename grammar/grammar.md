## 1. Parts of Speech

The eight traditional parts of speech classify words by their function in a sentence.

| Part of Speech | Function | Examples |
|----------------|----------|----------|
| Noun | Names a person, place, thing, or idea | *dog, London, freedom* |
| Pronoun | Replaces a noun | *he, she, it, they* |
| Verb | Expresses action or state of being | *run, is, think* |
| Adjective | Describes a noun | *happy, red, tall* |
| Adverb | Modifies a verb, adjective, or adverb | *quickly, very, well* |
| Preposition | Shows relationship between words | *in, on, under, before* |
| Conjunction | Joins words, phrases, or clauses | *and, but, because* |
| Interjection | Expresses emotion | *wow, ouch, hey* |

---

## 2. Nouns

A **noun** names a person, place, thing, or idea.

### Types of Nouns

- **Common noun** – a general name: *city, teacher, book*
- **Proper noun** – a specific name (capitalized): *Paris, Mr. Smith, Monday*
- **Concrete noun** – something perceivable by the senses: *apple, music, rain*
- **Abstract noun** – an idea or quality: *honesty, love, courage*
- **Collective noun** – a group: *team, flock, committee*
- **Countable noun** – can be counted: *one chair, two chairs*
- **Uncountable (mass) noun** – cannot be counted directly: *water, information, sand*

### Number

- **Singular**: *cat, child, mouse*
- **Plural**: *cats, children, mice*

Regular plurals add `-s` or `-es`. Irregular plurals change form (*man → men, foot → feet*).

### Possessives

- Singular: add `'s` → *the dog's bone*
- Plural ending in s: add `'` → *the dogs' bones*
- Plural not ending in s: add `'s` → *the children's toys*

---

## 3. Pronouns

A **pronoun** replaces a noun to avoid repetition.

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| Personal | I, you, he, she, it, we, they |
| Object | me, you, him, her, it, us, them |
| Possessive | mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs |
| Reflexive | myself, yourself, himself, themselves |
| Demonstrative | this, that, these, those |
| Interrogative | who, whom, whose, which, what |
| Relative | who, whom, whose, which, that |
| Indefinite | someone, anybody, everything, none, all |

**Antecedent:** the noun a pronoun refers to. The pronoun must agree in number and gender.
> *Maria lost her keys.* ("Maria" is the antecedent of "her".)

---

## 4. Verbs

A **verb** expresses an action, occurrence, or state of being.

### Types of Verbs

- **Action verb** – *run, write, jump*
- **Linking verb** – connects subject to a description: *be, seem, become, appear*
- **Auxiliary (helping) verb** – supports the main verb: *have, do, will, can, must*
- **Transitive verb** – takes a direct object: *She reads **books**.*
- **Intransitive verb** – takes no object: *He sleeps.*

### Principal Parts

| Base | Past | Past Participle | Present Participle |
|------|------|-----------------|--------------------|
| walk | walked | walked | walking |
| go | went | gone | going |
| write | wrote | written | writing |

### Regular vs. Irregular

- **Regular verbs** form the past with `-ed`: *play → played*
- **Irregular verbs** change form: *see → saw → seen*

---

## 5. Verb Tenses

Tenses show when an action occurs. English has 12 main tenses.

### Simple

- **Present:** *I write.*
- **Past:** *I wrote.*
- **Future:** *I will write.*

### Continuous (Progressive)

- **Present continuous:** *I am writing.*
- **Past continuous:** *I was writing.*
- **Future continuous:** *I will be writing.*

### Perfect

- **Present perfect:** *I have written.*
- **Past perfect:** *I had written.*
- **Future perfect:** *I will have written.*

### Perfect Continuous

- **Present perfect continuous:** *I have been writing.*
- **Past perfect continuous:** *I had been writing.*
- **Future perfect continuous:** *I will have been writing.*

## 6. Adjectives

An **adjective** describes or modifies a noun or pronoun.

### Order of Adjectives

Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Color → Origin → Material → Purpose
> *a lovely small old round brown Italian wooden serving bowl*

### Degrees of Comparison

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| tall | taller | tallest |
| good | better | best |
| beautiful | more beautiful | most beautiful |

---

## 7. Adverbs

An **adverb** modifies a verb, an adjective, another adverb, or even a whole sentence. Many are formed by adding `-ly` to an adjective (*quick → quickly*), but not all adverbs end in `-ly` (*fast, well, soon, here*), and not all `-ly` words are adverbs (*friendly, lovely* are adjectives).

### Types of Adverbs

| Type | Question answered | Examples | Sentence |
|------|-------------------|----------|----------|
| Manner | How? | *carefully, well, badly, quickly* | *She sang **beautifully**.* |
| Time | When? | *now, yesterday, soon, already, later* | *They left **yesterday**.* |
| Place | Where? | *here, there, everywhere, inside, abroad* | *Come **here**.* |
| Frequency | How often? | *always, usually, often, rarely, never* | *He **always** jogs.* |
| Degree | To what extent? | *very, quite, too, almost, enough* | *It was **very** cold.* |
| Purpose/Reason | Why? | *therefore, thus, consequently* | *It rained; **therefore**, we stayed in.* |

### Conjunctive Adverbs

These connect independent clauses and show logical relationships. When joining two clauses, they usually follow a semicolon and are followed by a comma.
> *The results were strong; **however**, more testing is needed.*

Common ones: *however, therefore, moreover, nevertheless, consequently, meanwhile, furthermore, otherwise, instead.*

### Sentence (Comment) Adverbs

These modify an entire sentence and often express the speaker's attitude.
> ***Fortunately**, no one was hurt.* / ***Honestly**, I don't know.*

Common ones: *fortunately, clearly, obviously, apparently, perhaps, surely, frankly.*

### Comparative and Superlative Adverbs

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| fast | faster | fastest |
| quickly | more quickly | most quickly |
| well | better | best |
| badly | worse | worst |

- Short adverbs usually add `-er / -est`: *soon → sooner → soonest.*
- Adverbs ending in `-ly` usually take *more / most*: *carefully → more carefully → most carefully.*

### Placement of Adverbs

- **Adverbs of frequency** typically go before the main verb but after *be*: *She **often** reads. / She is **often** late.*
- **Adverbs of manner** usually follow the verb or the object: *He drove **carefully**. / He read the letter **slowly**.*
- **Adverbs of degree** go before the word they modify: *a **very** large room.*
- Avoid placing an adverb between a verb and its direct object: *~~She reads quickly books~~* → *She reads books quickly.*

### Common Pitfalls

- **good vs. well:** *good* is an adjective; *well* is the adverb. *She sings **well*** (not *~~good~~*). Note: *I feel good/well* are both acceptable with slightly different meanings.
- **Flat adverbs:** some adverbs share the adjective form: *drive **fast**, work **hard**, aim **high**.* (*hardly* and *highly* have different meanings.)

## 8. Prepositions

A **preposition** links a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase (its *object*) to another part of the sentence, showing a relationship of place, time, direction, or logic.

### Common Prepositions by Category

| Category | Prepositions | Examples |
|----------|--------------|----------|
| Place/Position | *in, on, at, under, over, above, below, between, among, beside, behind, in front of* | *The book is **on** the table.* |
| Time | *at, on, in, before, after, during, since, until, for, by* | *We'll meet **at** noon.* |
| Direction/Movement | *to, into, onto, from, toward, across, through, along, past, up, down* | *She walked **across** the street.* |
| Agent/Means | *by, with* | *written **by** hand, cut **with** a knife* |
| Logic/Relation | *of, about, for, like, as, despite, due to* | *a story **about** courage* |

### Prepositions of Time: at / on / in

- **at** – precise times: *at 6 o'clock, at night, at noon*
- **on** – days and dates: *on Monday, on July 4th*
- **in** – months, years, seasons, longer periods: *in May, in 2026, in summer*

### Prepositions of Place: at / on / in

- **at** – a specific point: *at the door, at the bus stop*
- **on** – a surface or line: *on the wall, on the second floor*
- **in** – an enclosed space or area: *in the box, in Paris*

### Prepositional Phrases

A **prepositional phrase** = preposition + its object (+ any modifiers). It functions as an adjective or an adverb.
> *The cat **under the old wooden table** is mine.* (adjective — describes *cat*)
> *She arrived **after the meeting**.* (adverb — tells when)

### Phrasal Verbs vs. Prepositional Verbs

Many verbs combine with particles that look like prepositions:
- *look **after** (take care of), give **up** (quit), run **into** (meet by chance).*
The meaning often changes entirely, so learn them as whole units.

### Common Pitfalls

- **Ending a sentence with a preposition:** generally acceptable in modern English. *What are you looking **for**?* is natural and correct.
- **Redundant prepositions:** avoid doubling. *~~Where is it at?~~* → *Where is it?*
- **Fixed combinations (dependent prepositions):** certain words demand specific prepositions:
  - *interested **in**, good **at**, afraid **of**, depend **on**, consist **of**, different **from**, married **to**.*
- **in vs. into:** *in* shows position; *into* shows movement. *She was **in** the room. / She walked **into** the room.*
- **between vs. among:** use *between* for two (or distinct items), *among* for three or more as a group. *between you and me; among the crowd.*

## 9. Conjunctions

A **conjunction** joins words, phrases, or clauses.

- **Coordinating** (FANBOYS): *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so*
- **Subordinating:** *because, although, since, while, if, when*
- **Correlative** (pairs): *either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also*

---

## 10. Interjections

An **interjection** expresses sudden emotion, often standing alone with an exclamation point.
> *Wow! Ouch! Hooray! Oh no!*

---

## 11. Articles & Determiners

### Articles

- **Definite:** *the* (a specific thing)
- **Indefinite:** *a / an* (a non-specific thing). Use *a* before consonant sounds, *an* before vowel sounds.

### Determiners

Words that introduce nouns: *this, that, my, your, some, many, each, every, few.*

---

## 12. Sentence Structure

### The Four Sentence Types (by purpose)

- **Declarative:** states a fact. *The sky is blue.*
- **Interrogative:** asks a question. *Is the sky blue?*
- **Imperative:** gives a command. *Look at the sky.*
- **Exclamatory:** shows strong emotion. *What a beautiful sky!*

### The Four Sentence Structures

- **Simple:** one independent clause. *She runs.*
- **Compound:** two independent clauses joined by a conjunction. *She runs, and he walks.*
- **Complex:** one independent + one or more dependent clauses. *She runs because she enjoys it.*
- **Compound-complex:** two independent + at least one dependent clause. *She runs because she enjoys it, and he walks to relax.*

---

## 13. Clauses & Phrases

- **Clause:** a group of words with a subject and a verb.
  - **Independent clause:** can stand alone. *I left early.*
  - **Dependent (subordinate) clause:** cannot stand alone. *…because I was tired.*
- **Phrase:** a group of words without a subject–verb pair. *in the morning, running fast, the big red car.*

---

## 14. Subject–Verb Agreement

The verb must agree with its subject in number and person.

- Singular subject → singular verb: *The dog **barks**.*
- Plural subject → plural verb: *The dogs **bark**.*
- Compound subjects joined by *and* are usually plural: *Tom and Jerry **are** here.*
- With *or/nor*, the verb agrees with the nearer subject: *Neither the manager nor the employees **were** aware.*
- Indefinite pronouns like *everyone, each, nobody* are singular: *Everyone **is** welcome.*

---

## 15. Voice: Active & Passive

- **Active voice:** the subject performs the action. *The chef cooked the meal.*
- **Passive voice:** the subject receives the action. *The meal was cooked by the chef.*

Prefer active voice for clarity and directness; use passive when the actor is unknown or unimportant.

---

## 16. Mood

Mood expresses the speaker's attitude toward the action.

- **Indicative:** states facts or asks questions. *She is happy.*
- **Imperative:** gives commands. *Close the door.*
- **Subjunctive:** expresses wishes, hypotheticals, or demands. *If I **were** you… / I suggest that he **be** present.*

---

## 17. Punctuation

| Mark | Use | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| Period `.` | Ends a declarative sentence | *It rained.* |
| Comma `,` | Separates items, clauses, pauses | *red, white, and blue* |
| Semicolon `;` | Joins related independent clauses | *I came; I saw.* |
| Colon `:` | Introduces a list or explanation | *Bring these: pen, paper.* |
| Question mark `?` | Ends a question | *Who is it?* |
| Exclamation point `!` | Shows emotion | *Watch out!* |
| Apostrophe `'` | Possession and contractions | *dog's, don't* |
| Quotation marks `" "` | Direct speech, titles | *"Hello," she said.* |
| Hyphen `-` | Joins compound words | *well-known* |
| Dash `—` | Marks a break or emphasis | *He left — finally.* |
| Parentheses `( )` | Add extra information | *She (my sister) agreed.* |

### The Oxford (Serial) Comma

The comma before the final *and* in a list: *apples, oranges, and bananas.* It prevents ambiguity.

---

## 18. Capitalization

Capitalize:
- The first word of a sentence
- The pronoun *I*
- Proper nouns and proper adjectives: *France, French*
- Days, months, holidays: *Monday, July, Christmas*
- Titles when used with a name: *President Lincoln*
- The main words in titles of works: *The Lord of the Rings*

---

## 19. Common Grammar Mistakes

- **Run-on sentence:** two independent clauses with no proper connector. *I like tea I drink it daily.* → Fix with a period, semicolon, or conjunction.
- **Comma splice:** joining two independent clauses with only a comma. *It was late, I went home.* → Use a semicolon or conjunction.
- **Sentence fragment:** an incomplete sentence. *Because it was raining.*
- **Dangling modifier:** a modifier with no clear subject. *Walking to school, the rain started.*
- **Misplaced modifier:** a modifier positioned confusingly. *She almost drove the kids to school every day.*
- **Pronoun–antecedent disagreement:** *Everyone brought their book.* (debated; use *their* as a singular in informal writing, or rephrase.)

## 20. Commonly Confused Words

| Words | Difference |
|-------|------------|
| **your / you're** | possessive vs. "you are" |
| **its / it's** | possessive vs. "it is" |
| **their / there / they're** | possessive / place / "they are" |
| **to / too / two** | preposition / also / number |
| **affect / effect** | verb (to influence) / noun (a result) |
| **then / than** | time/sequence / comparison |
| **lose / loose** | to misplace / not tight |
| **accept / except** | to receive / excluding |
| **who / whom** | subject / object |
| **fewer / less** | countable / uncountable |
| **i.e. / e.g.** | "that is" / "for example" |
