# English Verbs by Frequency

A reference list of 1500+ English verbs, each shown with its four **principal parts** — base, past, past participle, and present participle — plus whether the verb is **regular** or **irregular**. Verbs are grouped into five tiers and ordered **approximately by frequency of usage**: the most common verbs appear in Tier 1, the rarest in Tier 5.

This file complements the grammar reference in `grammar.md`. For the grammatical rules governing verbs — the definition of the principal parts and the regular/irregular distinction — see **Section 4 (Verbs)** of that document; for how those forms combine into the twelve tenses, see **Section 5 (Verb Tenses)**.

---

## Legend

Each table has five columns:

- **Base** – the dictionary (infinitive) form: *walk, go, be*
- **Past** – the simple past tense: *walked, went, was/were*
- **Past Participle** – the form used with *have* (perfect tenses) and *be* (passive): *walked, gone, been*
- **Present Participle** – the `-ing` form used in continuous tenses and as a gerund: *walking, going, being*
- **Type** – **Regular** (past and past participle both formed with `-ed`) or **Irregular** (any other pattern)

Spelling reminders applied throughout: regular verbs add `-ed` (*play → played*); the present participle adds `-ing`, doubling a final consonant after a short stressed vowel (*run → running, refer → referring*), dropping a silent `-e` (*make → making*), and turning `-ie` into `-ying` (*die → dying*).

**How this file is organized:** verbs are split into five frequency tiers. **Tier 1 (Core)** holds the ~100 most common verbs, including the auxiliaries — these dominate everyday speech and writing and are mostly irregular. **Tier 2 (High Frequency)** adds ~250 everyday verbs needed for basic fluency. **Tier 3 (Common)** covers ~400 verbs frequent in general reading and conversation. **Tier 4 (Intermediate)** lists ~500 standard but less frequent verbs. **Tier 5 (Advanced)** gathers ~350 formal, literary, technical, or specialized verbs. Within each tier the ordering is approximate — more frequent verbs come first. No verb is repeated across tiers.
