## 1. Grammatical Types of Nouns

Before the thematic lists, here is a quick recap of how nouns are classified **grammatically**. The lists later in this file are organized by **meaning** (semantic category), but every noun in them also belongs to one or more of these grammatical types.

- **Common noun** – a general name: *city, teacher, book*
- **Proper noun** – a specific, capitalized name: *Paris, Monday, Mr. Smith*
- **Concrete noun** – perceivable by the senses: *apple, music, rain*
- **Abstract noun** – an idea or quality: *honesty, love, courage* (see Section 16)
- **Collective noun** – names a group: *team, flock, committee*
- **Countable noun** – can be counted: *one chair, two chairs*
- **Uncountable (mass) noun** – cannot be counted directly: *water, information, sand*

> For full definitions, plus rules on singular/plural number and possessives, see **Section 2 of `grammar.md`**.

**How the rest of this file is organized:** nouns are grouped into 17 semantic categories (people, body, animals, food, and so on). Within each category the ordering is approximate — frequent, everyday words come first; more specialized vocabulary follows.
